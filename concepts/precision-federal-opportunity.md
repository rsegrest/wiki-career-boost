## The objections, in order of how much they should slow him down

1. **⚠️ Sign the agreement *before* the first conversation.** This is backwards, and it is the single
   clearest process problem. A one-page "welcome agreement" that precedes any human conversation has not
   been read by anyone who could answer a question about it. **Talk first, sign second** — the order in
   the email is a negotiating posture, not a normal hiring sequence.
2. **⚠️ Conflict of interest with Amentum/NASA is a real, specific case — not a formality.** Rick's
   moonlighting position (established 2026-11-01) is that outside work is **allowed with manager approval
   and no conflict of interest**. Federal contractor to federal contractor, with a firm that bids on
   federal work and names him on proposals, is exactly the case that approval exists for. **Manager
   approval in writing is a prerequisite, not a follow-up.**
3. **Being "kept on proposals" implies unpaid proposal labour.** The email says he'll be told monthly which
   proposals he is on, and that work begins *after* a win. Whether proposal support is paid is unstated and
   must be asked. Naming him on a bid also puts his resume into a government proposal.
4. **1099 vs W-2 is unstated**, and it is worth ~$470/mo at 5 hr/wk. It also decides whether the 38.6%
   self-employment reserve applies — see the treatment note in the budget page.
5. **IP, exclusivity, non-solicit and liability are unseen.** A one-page agreement can still assign
   inventions. Given Rick has an in-flight venture ([[concepts/rcca-app-venture]]) and a flagship skill
   project, an overbroad IP assignment is the one term that could actually cost him something.
6. **The work is federal — which is the buyer Rick ruled out for AI work.** Precision markets itself as
   "production AI for federal agencies". The *roles* on offer (full-stack, modelling/simulation) need not
   be AI-feature work, but the practical consequence is that **on a DOJ/CDC engagement he probably cannot
   use the AI tooling that currently makes him fast**, so $90/hr buys materially less output than his
   recent rate of building. That is a real, if acceptable, discount.
7. **Verify the SignWell link before opening it.** It arrived as a raw `signwell.com/docs/...` URL in an
   email. Anyone can create a SignWell document. Confirm the sender domain and read the terms in the
   browser before signing anything.
