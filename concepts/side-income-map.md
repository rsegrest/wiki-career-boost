## The agency subcontract thread — and why generic overflow fails here (2026-11-01)

Rick approved exploring this. **The first pass of research changed the shape of it, so read this before
pitching anyone.** White-label / overflow dev shops are a saturated market: the visible band is **€23–50/hr
(~$25–55)** for frontend, backend and full-stack, with **senior AI/DevOps profiles topping out around
$60/hr** — offshore and nearshore teams, NDA-first, staffed in 3–7 days, no lock-in. That is a commodity
market selling *bodies*, and Rick cannot win it on availability.

**The arithmetic of why that matters:**

| Positioning | Rate | Hours/mo to net $900 (the treatment cost-share) |
|---|---|---|
| Generic overflow, offshore band | ~$45/hr | **32.6 hr/mo = 7.5 hr/wk** — above his ceiling |
| Top of that band (senior AI profile) | ~$60/hr | **24.4 hr/mo = 5.6 hr/wk** — still above it |
| His target | $125/hr | **11.7 hr/mo = 2.7 hr/wk** — fits |

**So sell the deliverable, not the hours.** Three shapes that survive the competition, all fixed-scope:

1. **AI code remediation** — the strongest gap. Offshore shops now ship AI features; a large share land
   badly (hallucinating retrieval, no eval, no guardrails, prompt-injection exposure). "Your offshore team
   shipped it; I'll make it behave" is a $125/hr job the offshore band cannot underbid, because it requires
   judgement rather than throughput.
2. **Fixed-scope AI feature build** — an agency has already sold the project; he builds one scoped module
   under their brand.
3. **AI-integration assessment** — the one-day paid diagnostic already on the offer ladder (~$750).

**Who to approach:** the *seller* of AI work who cannot *staff* it — AI-automation consultancies, digital
agencies with an "AI practice" they are faking, and **MSPs**, who sell IT to SMBs and now get asked for AI
work they cannot build. Directories (Clutch, Sortlist, Expertise) are a **starting list, not qualified
prospects** — nothing there has been screened for the commercial-only AI rule.

## Ruled out, with the reasons (so they do not come back)

| Idea | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Investing** to cover a cash-flow deficit | The debt costs ~6.8%; safe yields ~4%. Paying the debt *is* the better risk-adjusted position |
| **Buying and reselling** online | Needs working capital that costs 6.8% here, and ranks below capital-free work |
| **Power Apps / low-code** consulting | Rick does not want to do it, government tenants constrain it, and it is capability rather than an offer |
| **AI work for government or defence networks** | IA review, no external model calls, no data egress - the slowest possible first dollar |
| **Bidding on freelance marketplaces** | Medians run ~$27-30/hr, a quarter of the target rate. Platforms are discovery, not the rate |
| **Toptal** | One failed screen, no retry |
| **Generic overflow / white-label dev subcontracting** | A commodity market at €23-50/hr staffing bodies in 3-7 days. Netting the $900/mo treatment cost would need 7.5 hr/wk, above Rick's ceiling. Only the *deliverable* versions survive — see the agency note above |
| **Forming a 501(c)(3), or a foundation-owned subsidiary** | The $283-825 first-year fees are trivial; the real cost is forfeiting ownership permanently, plus 990s, state charitable registrations and a board. Not required in order to accept donations - see [[concepts/open-source-business-model]] |
