# Freelance Outreach Targets

> **Why this page exists:** it fulfils the Path C checklist item "identify 10 local/regional companies
> that might need AI integration", and answers the only question that stands between Rick and the
> first paid hour: *who do I actually talk to?* Strategy lives in

## Read this first: the rate reality (checked 2026-10-11)

- **Marketplaces pay a median of ~$27-30/hr for AI/automation work** (Upwork-derived samples of 5,536 jobs
  and 18,427 posts). The "$150/hr AI freelancer" figure is a myth *on platforms*.
- Demand is genuinely rising — Upwork's 2026 report shows AI skill demand **+109% YoY** and AI-enabled
  freelancers earning **~40% more per hour** — but that premium lands for people who already have a
  relationship, not for whoever bids lowest.
- Upwork's talent fee is now a **0-15% variable** rate (since May 2025), not the flat 10% most guides quote.
- **Consequence:** the $120-175/hr band this plan needs does not exist in platform bidding. Platforms
  are a *discovery and credibility* channel; **the rate comes from direct relationships.** That makes
  this list the rate-critical path, not a nice-to-have.

## Tier 1 — warm: someone already trusts you

**1. The LinkedIn 1st-degree "moved commercial" sweep.** Filter contacts who left government contracting
for commercial product or software firms. They already know the engineering is real, so the ask is
favour-sized rather than a sales call.
*How:* 30 minutes scrolling, list 15-20 names, then message in batches of five.

**2. Former NASA/Amentum colleagues who now hold budgets.** The same list, filtered for
lead / manager / founder / CTO titles — the people with a backlog they cannot staff.

**3. Referral partners who each serve 50-200 SMBs.** Accountants, bookkeepers, business bankers,
insurance agents, and especially **MSPs** — they sell small businesses their IT and cannot build AI.
One relationship produces many introductions, because they are asked "who can build us X?" constantly.
*How:* 20 minutes with one accountant you already know; propose a referral arrangement.

## Beyond Huntsville: which metros, and why (2026-10-11)

**The frame first.** Delivery is remote, so distance does not gate *doing* the work — it gates
*relationship range*. A metro he can drive to in ~2 hours is one he can attend events in, buy lunch in,
and get introduced in. A metro 3.5 hours away is a conference or a day trip. So the tiers below are
relationship tiers, not delivery tiers.

**And a third criterion, added by Rick 2026-10-11: will he actually go?** A market he does not
want to travel to is a market he will not travel to, however good its sector mix. Travel appetite is a
real input to market selection, not a soft preference.

**And the defence problem is sector-shaped, not city-shaped.** Every metro's defence employers are the
same bad buyer for AI features (IA review, no external model calls, no data egress). What actually varies
is the **ratio of commercial, document-heavy employers** — healthcare, insurance, financial services,
logistics, professional services. That ratio, plus competition and drivability, is what ranks these.

**The multiplier that beats city-by-city prospecting: join two regional industry associations, not ten
cities' worth of directories.** One healthcare, insurance, legal or staffing association membership in
Birmingham or Nashville puts him in front of dozens of commercial buyers in the right sector at once —
and associations are where SMB owners already gather. Conferences in Birmingham, Nashville or Chattanooga
are the cheapest way to meet many commercial buyers in a single day.

### The two sector plays

1. **Insurance document workflows.** Unum (~$13.3B revenue) and BlueCross BlueShield of Tennessee
   (~$6.2B) are Chattanooga's two largest companies, and insurance is the archetypal document-heavy,
   commercial, non-defence buyer: claims, policy documents, intake, correspondence.
2. **Freight and logistics operations.** Kenco, U.S. Xpress and Covenant Logistics locally — and the
   sector is document-saturated: bills of lading, rate confirmations, claims, customs paperwork, driver
   documents. Extraction and assistant work is a natural fit, and every buyer is commercial.

### The event that matters

FreightTech Awards dinner. One drivable event with the entire freight-tech ecosystem in his chosen city.
*(Dates from a third-party event listing — verify with FreightWaves before booking.)*

**And a pre-built prospect list:** FreightWaves publishes the **FreightTech100** (announced Sept 2026) —
100 named freight and supply-chain technology companies. A target list that already exists, in a sector
where his offer is differentiated, published by a company headquartered in his chosen market.

### First three moves

1. **Attend one ChaTech or Chamber event before joining anything** — confirm the room matches the pitch.
2. **Apply to CO.LAB Office Hours as the AI expert** — recurring, low effort, expert-positioned, and it
   generates inbound founder referrals.
3. **Pick one sector and write one offer for it** — insurance document workflows *or* freight document
   workflows — then use the FreightTech100 for names.



**The credibility bridge.** The NASA/TBE pedigree is the *hook*, but the *buyer* has to be commercial:
aerospace-grade rigor applied to commercial document workflows. Sell the discipline, not the sector.

## Do not spend hours here

- **Government/defence networks as AI buyers** — IA review, no external model calls, no data egress.
  The slowest possible first dollar. See [[concepts/freelance-portfolio-strategy]].
- **Toptal** — one failed screen, no retry.
- **Power Apps / low-code** — capability, not an offer.
- **Bidding wars on marketplaces** — priced at the median, a quarter of the target rate.

## The opening message (one sentence, and it qualifies the buyer)

> "I build AI features on commercial networks — assistants over your own documents, extraction from
> forms, workflow automation — and nothing has to leave your network. Two weeks, fixed fee. If you have
> a document-heavy workflow, I'll show you the one-week version."

## Cadence that fits the plan's ceiling

Five warm touches a week inside a **4 hr/wk cap**: three messages, one event or clinic, one follow-up.
Outreach is the unpaid ramp, so it gets the *calendar*, not extra hours.
