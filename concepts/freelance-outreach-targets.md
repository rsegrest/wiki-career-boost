# Freelance Outreach Targets

> **Why this page exists:** it fulfils the Path C checklist item "identify 10 local/regional companies
> that might need AI integration", and answers the only question that stands between Rick and the
> first paid hour: *who do I actually talk to?* Strategy lives in
> [[concepts/freelance-portfolio-strategy]]; the money math in
> [[../../wiki-personal/concepts/near-term-income-plan]].

## Read this first: the rate reality (checked 2026-10-11)

- **Marketplaces pay a median of ~$27-30/hr for AI/automation work** (Upwork-derived samples of 5,536 jobs
  and 18,427 posts). The "$150/hr AI freelancer" figure is a myth *on platforms*.
- Demand is genuinely rising — Upwork's 2026 report shows AI skill demand **+109% YoY** and AI-enabled
  freelancers earning **~40% more per hour** — but that premium lands for people who already have a
  relationship, not for whoever bids lowest.
- Upwork's talent fee is now a **0-15% variable** rate (since May 2025), not the flat 10% most guides quote.
- **Consequence:** the $120-175/hr band this plan needs does not exist in platform bidding. Platforms
  are a *discovery and credibility* channel; **the rate comes from direct relationships.** That makes
  this list the rate-critical path, not a nice-to-have.

## Tier 1 — warm: someone already trusts you

**1. The LinkedIn 1st-degree "moved commercial" sweep.** Filter contacts who left government contracting
for commercial product or software firms. They already know the engineering is real, so the ask is
favour-sized rather than a sales call.
*How:* 30 minutes scrolling, list 15-20 names, then message in batches of five.

**2. Former NASA/Amentum colleagues who now hold budgets.** The same list, filtered for
lead / manager / founder / CTO titles — the people with a backlog they cannot staff.

**3. Referral partners who each serve 50-200 SMBs.** Accountants, bookkeepers, business bankers,
insurance agents, and especially **MSPs** — they sell small businesses their IT and cannot build AI.
One relationship produces many introductions, because they are asked "who can build us X?" constantly.
*How:* 20 minutes with one accountant you already know; propose a referral arrangement.

**8. Local professional services — law, medical, staffing, real estate, insurance agencies.** The
natural buyers of document AI (intake, extraction, summarisation), and commercial by definition: no CUI,
no accreditation, no IA review. *How:* partner-level direct outreach naming the specific workflow
(intake forms, discovery documents, patient records, contracts).

## Tier 3 — product companies and the community

**9. Commercial software/SaaS with a React frontend and no AI features.** LinkedIn company search:
20-200 employees, product built in React, no AI in the feature list. This is the existing Week 3 item,
and the offer is literally "AI features wired into the tool you already have."

**10. The AI/agent community you are already visible in (X + LinkedIn).** The play is not pitching —
it is **the arcade-skill launch as public proof of work**, which converts attention into inbound DMs.
That credibility engine is what makes 1-9 land instead of bounce.

## Do not spend hours here

- **Government/defence networks as AI buyers** — IA review, no external model calls, no data egress.
  The slowest possible first dollar. See [[concepts/freelance-portfolio-strategy]].
- **Toptal** — one failed screen, no retry.
- **Power Apps / low-code** — capability, not an offer.
- **Bidding wars on marketplaces** — priced at the median, a quarter of the target rate.

## The opening message (one sentence, and it qualifies the buyer)

> "I build AI features on commercial networks — assistants over your own documents, extraction from
> forms, workflow automation — and nothing has to leave your network. Two weeks, fixed fee. If you have
> a document-heavy workflow, I'll show you the one-week version."

## Cadence that fits the plan's ceiling

Five warm touches a week inside a **4 hr/wk cap**: three messages, one event or clinic, one follow-up.
Outreach is the unpaid ramp, so it gets the *calendar*, not extra hours.
